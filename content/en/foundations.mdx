## The idea

A population describes the process we care about. A sample is one set of observations from it. An estimate is a number computed from a sample, so another sample usually gives another estimate. Probability gives us a language for this uncertainty.

## The mathematics

$$
E[X]=\sum_x xP(X=x),\qquad \operatorname{Var}(X)=E[(X-E[X])^2]
$$

## Try this experiment

Imagine estimating the fraction of students who cycle to university. Ask whether your sample covers commuters, residential students, and different days. A larger biased sample can remain biased.

## Know the limits

Expectation need not be a possible observation: the mean of a fair die is 3.5. Independence is a property of a joint distribution, not a synonym for zero correlation.
