## The idea

The E-step computes posterior responsibilities using the current parameters. The M-step updates weights, means and covariances using these responsibilities. Unlike K-means, each point can partially belong to several components. The chart blends colors according to these probabilities.

## The mathematics

$$
\gamma_{ik}=\frac{\pi_k\mathcal N(x_i\mid\mu_k,\Sigma_k)}{\sum_j\pi_j\mathcal N(x_i\mid\mu_j,\Sigma_j)}
$$

## Try this experiment

Move through completed EM iterations and inspect the observed log-likelihood. Change the initialization seed and fitted component count. The generated data has three groups even when you fit a different number.

## Know the limits

Exact EM does not decrease the likelihood, but does not guarantee a global optimum. Gaussian mixtures can become singular; this implementation floors covariance eigenvalues. Numerical safeguards mean tiny likelihood changes should be interpreted with tolerance.
